# Business Insights and Recommendations

## 1. Objective

The objective of this document is to translate the NovaRetail analytical results into business insights and potential decision-support recommendations.

While the Power BI dashboard provides an interactive environment for exploring sales performance, this document focuses on the interpretation of the main patterns identified in the data.

The analysis covers four main perspectives:

- overall business performance;
- product and store performance;
- salesperson and sales team performance;
- temporal sales patterns.

The analysis follows a structured approach:

**observation → interpretation → recommendation**

Observations are based on measurable results from the NovaRetail dataset.

Interpretations explain the potential business meaning of these results without presenting assumptions as established facts.

Recommendations are proposed only when the available analysis provides sufficient evidence to support a potential business action.

The objective is therefore not only to identify what happened, but also to determine which findings deserve further investigation or could support business decision-making.

## 2. Overall Business Performance

NovaRetail generated **€4.25M in revenue** across the 2023–2025 reporting period, with approximately **€956K in gross margin** and an overall **gross margin rate of 22.5%**.

The dataset contains **10,000 sales transactions**, representing **22,795 units sold**.

### Revenue Growth

Revenue increased throughout the three-year period:

| Year |        Revenue | YoY Growth |
| ---- | -------------: | ---------: |
| 2023 | €1,152,026.37 |         — |
| 2024 | €1,467,733.13 |    +27.40% |
| 2025 | €1,630,072.05 |    +11.06% |

Revenue therefore increased in both 2024 and 2025.

However, the annual growth rate decreased from **27.40% in 2024 to 11.06% in 2025**.

### Business Interpretation

The results indicate that NovaRetail continued to expand its revenue in 2025, but the pace of growth slowed substantially compared with the previous year.

This slowdown does not, by itself, identify a business problem or its cause.

Further analysis is required to determine whether the change in growth is associated with specific stores, product categories, sales activity, or temporal patterns.

This question is explored through the more detailed analytical perspectives presented in the following sections.

## 3. Product and Store Insights

Product and store performance reveals an important distinction between revenue generation and profitability.

### Store Performance

Revenue performance varies significantly across NovaRetail stores.

Paris République is the leading store, generating approximately **€619K in revenue**, followed by Paris La Défense with approximately **€520K** and Lyon Part-Dieu with approximately **€497K**.

The five highest-revenue stores collectively generate approximately **59% of total company revenue**.

However, differences in gross margin rate are much smaller than differences in revenue. Across the ten stores, gross margin rates range from approximately **21.8% to 23.5%**, compared with an overall company rate of **22.5%**.

For example, Paris République generates the highest revenue while maintaining a gross margin rate of **22.3%**. Strasbourg Centre generates the lowest revenue, at approximately **€275K**, but records the highest gross margin rate at **23.5%**.

#### Business Interpretation

Store performance differences therefore appear to be driven primarily by differences in revenue generation rather than major differences in relative profitability.

A high-revenue store is not necessarily the store with the highest gross margin rate.

This suggests that store performance should be evaluated using both revenue contribution and profitability rather than through revenue rankings alone.

### Product Category Performance

The product analysis shows a much stronger contrast between revenue and profitability.

The highest-revenue categories are dominated by technology products:

- **Smartphone:** approximately €1.04M in revenue with a 12.2% gross margin rate;
- **4K Television:** approximately €895K with a 17.4% margin rate;
- **Laptop:** approximately €777K with a 15.4% margin rate;
- **Bluetooth Headphones:** approximately €532K with a 32.7% margin rate.

By comparison, several lower-revenue fashion categories generate substantially higher gross margin rates. Bags, sneakers, jackets, and jeans all achieve gross margin rates above approximately **42%**.

This demonstrates that the categories generating the most revenue are not necessarily those generating the strongest profitability relative to sales.

### Product-Level Performance

The same pattern is visible at individual product level.

The highest-revenue products are primarily televisions, smartphones, and laptops. Together, the products displayed in the revenue ranking generate approximately **€1.37M in revenue**, with an aggregate gross margin rate of approximately **14.5%**.

The gross-margin ranking presents a different product mix.

Products such as Adidas Stan Smith, Dior Sauvage, and Lancôme La Vie Est Belle generate substantially less revenue individually but achieve gross margin rates above **43%**, allowing them to make a significant contribution to gross margin relative to their sales value.

#### Business Interpretation

These results show that product performance cannot be evaluated through revenue alone.

High-revenue technology products make a major contribution to overall sales, while several fashion and beauty products provide much stronger relative profitability.

The two groups therefore play different roles in the product portfolio.

Rather than automatically favoring one group over the other, portfolio analysis should consider both:

- **revenue contribution**, which reflects the scale of sales activity;
- **gross margin contribution and gross margin rate**, which reflect absolute and relative profitability.

### Potential Decision-Support Implication

NovaRetail should monitor product and category performance using both revenue and profitability indicators.

The analysis does not provide sufficient evidence to recommend reducing high-revenue, lower-margin categories or expanding specific high-margin categories automatically.

However, the strong differences in margin structure suggest that further analysis of the product mix, discounting, and the balance between high-revenue and high-margin products could support future commercial decisions.

## 4. Sales Performance Insights

Sales performance varies significantly across individual salespeople, both in terms of revenue contribution and sales activity.

### Salesperson Performance

Lefèvre is the leading salesperson, generating approximately **€861K in revenue**, representing about **20% of total NovaRetail revenue**.

Lefèvre also records the highest sales activity, with **1,000 transactions** and **4,674 units sold**.

The second-highest revenue contributor, Moreau, generates approximately **€543K**, followed by Dubois with approximately **€486K**.

This indicates a substantial difference in revenue contribution across the salesforce.

However, revenue leadership does not correspond directly to the highest gross margin rate.

Lefèvre achieves a gross margin rate of **22.6%**, close to the overall company rate of **22.5%**, while Girard records the highest gross margin rate among the salespeople at **24.1%** despite generating approximately **€162K in revenue**.

At the other end of the profitability range, Bonnet records a gross margin rate of **21.1%**.

### Revenue and Profitability Profiles

The comparison between revenue and gross margin rate highlights different salesperson performance profiles.

Some salespeople generate above-average revenue while maintaining profitability close to or above the company average, while others generate lower revenue but achieve relatively stronger margin rates.

The average reference lines included in the Power BI scatter plot help identify these different profiles without evaluating salesperson performance through revenue alone.

### Business Interpretation

The results suggest that differences between salespeople are driven more strongly by revenue contribution and sales activity than by large differences in gross margin rate.

Revenue varies substantially across the salesforce, while most gross margin rates remain relatively close to the overall company level.

This means that salesperson performance should be evaluated using multiple indicators, including:

- revenue contribution;
- gross margin;
- gross margin rate;
- transaction activity;
- quantity sold.

A revenue-only ranking would not fully capture differences in profitability or commercial activity.

### Potential Decision-Support Implication

The strong differences in revenue contribution justify further investigation into the factors associated with the highest-performing salespeople.

For example, management could examine whether differences are associated with customer portfolios, product mix, store activity, transaction frequency, or quantities sold.

The available results identify performance differences but do not establish their underlying causes.

### Sales Team Performance

Performance differences are also visible at sales team level.

The **North Team** generates approximately **€2.71M in revenue**, representing about **64% of total NovaRetail revenue**. It also records the highest level of sales activity, with **4,400 transactions** and **14,603 units sold**.

By comparison, the South Team generates approximately **€858K in revenue**, while the West Team generates approximately **€687K**.

Despite these substantial differences in revenue and sales activity, gross margin rates remain very similar across the three teams:

- **North Team:** 22.6%;
- **South Team:** 22.3%;
- **West Team:** 22.4%.

This indicates that the difference in team performance is primarily associated with the scale of sales activity rather than major differences in relative profitability.

The strong revenue concentration within the North Team also reflects the presence of several of the highest-revenue salespeople, including Lefèvre, Moreau, Dubois, Fontaine, and Lambert.

This concentration should not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the North Team is intrinsically more effective. Differences in customer portfolios, product mix, transaction activity, or other commercial conditions may also contribute to the observed performance.

## 5. Time-Based Insights

The time-based analysis highlights both long-term revenue growth and recurring differences in daily sales performance.

### Annual Revenue Growth

NovaRetail revenue increased throughout the three-year reporting period.

Revenue increased from approximately **€1.15M in 2023** to **€1.47M in 2024**, representing year-over-year growth of **27.40%**.

Revenue continued to increase in 2025, reaching approximately **€1.63M**. However, year-over-year growth slowed to **11.06%**.

This confirms that the business continued to grow, but at a substantially slower rate in 2025 than in 2024.

### Weekday vs Weekend Performance

Average daily revenue differs between weekdays and weekends.

- **Weekdays:** €3,768.72 average daily revenue;
- **Weekends:** €4,149.93 average daily revenue.

Average daily revenue is therefore approximately **10% higher during weekends** than during weekdays.

Using average daily revenue rather than total revenue provides a normalized comparison because the calendar contains more weekdays than weekend days.

### Day-of-Week Performance

The day-of-week analysis provides additional detail:

| Day       | Average Daily Revenue |
| --------- | --------------------: |
| Monday    |            €4,008.30 |
| Tuesday   |            €3,882.18 |
| Wednesday |            €3,569.28 |
| Thursday  |            €3,688.84 |
| Friday    |            €3,693.99 |
| Saturday  |            €4,057.01 |
| Sunday    |            €4,242.26 |

**Sunday records the highest average daily revenue at approximately €4.24K**, followed by Saturday at approximately €4.06K.

Wednesday records the lowest average daily revenue at approximately **€3.57K**.

The results therefore indicate that NovaRetail's daily sales performance is generally stronger during the weekend, particularly on Sunday.

### Business Interpretation

The combination of the weekday/weekend comparison and the individual day analysis suggests a recurring weekly sales pattern.

Weekend days generate stronger average daily revenue, while several midweek days generate lower daily revenue.

This pattern may be relevant for operational and commercial planning. However, the available data does not establish why weekend performance is stronger.

Potential contributing factors could include customer availability, store traffic, product mix, or purchasing behavior, but these explanations would require additional data to validate.

### Potential Decision-Support Implication

NovaRetail could use the observed weekly pattern to investigate whether commercial resources, staffing, promotions, or inventory availability are appropriately aligned with periods of stronger customer activity.

The results do not justify automatically reallocating resources, but they provide a clear basis for further operational analysis, particularly around weekend activity.

## 6. Key Business Findings

The analysis highlights six main findings across NovaRetail's sales performance.

### 1. Revenue Continues to Grow, but Growth Is Slowing

NovaRetail revenue increased from approximately **€1.15M in 2023** to **€1.47M in 2024** and **€1.63M in 2025**.

However, year-over-year growth decreased from **27.40% in 2024 to 11.06% in 2025**.

The company therefore continues to grow, but the pace of revenue expansion has slowed significantly.

### 2. Revenue Leadership Does Not Automatically Mean Higher Profitability

This pattern appears across several analytical dimensions.

Stores and salespeople with the highest revenue do not necessarily achieve the highest gross margin rates.

The distinction is even stronger at product level, where several high-revenue technology categories operate with substantially lower margin rates than lower-revenue fashion and beauty categories.

Performance should therefore be evaluated using both revenue and profitability indicators.

### 3. The Product Portfolio Contains Different Performance Profiles

Technology categories such as smartphones, televisions, and laptops make a major contribution to total revenue but operate with relatively low gross margin rates.

By comparison, several fashion and beauty products generate much lower revenue but substantially higher margin rates.

These product groups therefore contribute differently to NovaRetail's overall commercial and profitability performance.

### 4. Sales Performance Is Concentrated Among Specific Salespeople and Teams

Lefèvre generates approximately **20% of total company revenue**, while the North Team generates approximately **64%**.

However, gross margin rates remain relatively close across the three sales teams.

The observed differences in team performance therefore appear to be associated primarily with the scale of sales activity rather than major differences in relative profitability.

### 5. Store Revenue Varies More Than Store Profitability

Revenue differs substantially across the ten stores, ranging from approximately **€275K to €619K**.

Gross margin rates are much more stable, ranging from approximately **21.8% to 23.5%**.

This indicates that store-level differences are driven primarily by revenue generation rather than large differences in margin rate.

### 6. Weekend Sales Performance Is Stronger on a Daily Basis

Average daily revenue is approximately **€4.15K during weekends**, compared with approximately **€3.77K during weekdays**.

Sunday records the highest average daily revenue at approximately **€4.24K**, while Wednesday records the lowest at approximately **€3.57K**.

The results indicate a recurring weekly pattern in which weekend activity is stronger on a daily basis.

## 7. Recommendations

The analysis identifies several areas where NovaRetail could deepen its decision-making process.

These recommendations are intended as decision-support directions rather than definitive actions, since the available dataset identifies performance patterns but does not establish all of their underlying causes.

### 1. Investigate the Slowdown in Revenue Growth

Revenue continued to increase in 2025, but year-over-year growth slowed from **27.40% to 11.06%**.

NovaRetail should investigate the drivers of this slowdown by decomposing revenue growth across stores, product categories, transaction activity, and quantities sold.

The objective would be to determine whether the slowdown is broadly distributed across the business or concentrated in specific areas.

This analysis should be completed before deciding whether corrective commercial actions are required.

### 2. Manage Product Performance Using Both Revenue and Profitability

Product portfolio decisions should not rely on revenue rankings alone.

High-revenue technology categories make a major contribution to sales but generally operate with lower gross margin rates, while several fashion and beauty products generate stronger relative profitability.

NovaRetail should therefore monitor product and category performance using a combination of:

- revenue;
- gross margin;
- gross margin rate;
- quantity sold;
- discount activity.

This multidimensional approach would provide a more balanced basis for product portfolio and commercial decisions.

### 3. Investigate the Drivers of Salesperson and Team Performance

Sales performance is strongly concentrated among specific salespeople and within the North Team.

Rather than interpreting these differences as individual effectiveness alone, NovaRetail should investigate potential contributing factors such as customer portfolio, product mix, transaction frequency, quantities sold, and store context.

Understanding these drivers could help determine whether successful commercial patterns can be reproduced elsewhere or whether the observed differences are primarily explained by different operating conditions.

### 4. Evaluate Commercial and Operational Alignment with Weekend Activity

Average daily revenue is approximately **10% higher during weekends than during weekdays**, with Sunday recording the highest average daily revenue.

NovaRetail should investigate whether staffing, inventory availability, and commercial activity are appropriately aligned with this recurring pattern.

Before reallocating resources, additional operational data such as customer traffic, staffing levels, stock availability, and transaction timing would be required to determine whether changes would generate measurable business value.

### Recommendation Principle

The analysis supports targeted investigation rather than immediate prescriptive action.

The dashboard identifies **where meaningful performance differences exist**, while additional business and operational data would often be required to establish **why they exist** and determine the most appropriate response.

## 8. Analytical Limitations

The NovaRetail analysis provides a structured view of sales performance, but several limitations should be considered when interpreting the results.

### Portfolio Dataset

NovaRetail is a simulated business dataset created for this BI portfolio project.

The observed patterns are therefore intended to demonstrate analytical methods and decision-support reasoning rather than represent the performance of a real company.

### Limited Business Context

The dataset contains detailed sales information but does not include all of the operational and commercial factors that could explain performance differences.

For example, the analysis does not include information about:

- customer traffic;
- staffing levels and working hours;
- inventory availability and stock-outs;
- marketing campaigns;
- competitor activity;
- store operating costs.

As a result, the analysis can identify performance differences but cannot always determine their underlying causes.

### Correlation Does Not Establish Causation

Observed relationships should not automatically be interpreted as causal relationships.

For example, stronger weekend revenue does not demonstrate that increasing weekend staffing would increase sales.

Similarly, differences between salesperson or store performance may be associated with factors such as customer portfolio, product mix, location, or transaction activity.

Additional data would be required to test these hypotheses.

### Profitability Scope

Profitability analysis is based on product-level gross margin calculated from sales revenue and product cost.

It does not represent full operating profitability because expenses such as salaries, rent, logistics, marketing, and other operating costs are not included.

The gross margin indicators should therefore be interpreted as measures of product and sales profitability rather than net business profitability.

### Analytical Scope

The dashboard is designed primarily for descriptive and diagnostic analysis.

It identifies what happened and highlights areas that may require further investigation.

Predictive forecasting, causal analysis, and optimization models are outside the current scope of the project and would require additional data and analytical methods.

## 9. Conclusion

The NovaRetail analysis demonstrates how a structured BI approach can transform transactional sales data into actionable decision-support information.

Across the 2023–2025 period, the analysis identified several important patterns:

- revenue continued to increase, although year-over-year growth slowed in 2025;
- revenue contribution and profitability do not always follow the same pattern across products, stores, and salespeople;
- sales performance is concentrated among specific salespeople and teams;
- store performance differences are driven more strongly by revenue generation than by major differences in gross margin rate;
- weekend activity generates higher average daily revenue than weekday activity.

These findings demonstrate the importance of analyzing business performance through multiple complementary indicators rather than relying on revenue alone.

The resulting recommendations focus primarily on further investigation of the identified performance differences before prescriptive business actions are taken.

Together with the PostgreSQL data model, SQL analysis, Power BI semantic model, and interactive dashboard, this business analysis completes the NovaRetail BI workflow:

**data modeling → data quality → SQL analysis → semantic modeling → visualization → business interpretation → decision support**

The project therefore demonstrates an end-to-end approach to Business Intelligence, from structured data preparation to the interpretation of analytical results for business decision-making.
